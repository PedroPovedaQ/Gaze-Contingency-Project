# XR Search Tasks and Self-Similar Voice Literature Inventory

**Status:** Literature discovery record, not an exhaustive systematic review.

## Scope and search method

This inventory supports the Gaze Contingency study design. The search covered work published from 1995 through August 2026 using combinations of *visual search*, *object search*, *target search*, *out-of-view*, *attention guidance*, *virtual reality*, *augmented reality*, *mixed reality*, *self-similar voice*, *own voice*, *voice similarity*, and *voice cloning*. Sources included ISMAR, IEEE VR/VRW, CHI, SUI, VRST, MobileHCI, CHI PLAY, HFES, PACMHCI, IEEE TVCG, and relevant perception/HCI journals. Discovery used proceedings pages, OpenAlex, title and DOI searches, and citation chaining from the 2024 ISMAR out-of-view review.

The word *search* is overloaded. Computer-vision papers about image retrieval, tracking, or optimization were excluded unless a human participant searched for information or a target in XR. Clinical visual-field tests and information-retrieval interfaces are listed separately from object-search experiments. A paper is marked **Direct** when its participant task closely resembles finding a target among distractors in an HMD; **Transferable** when it tests a relevant cue, display constraint, or search behavior; and **Adjacent** when it mainly contributes a method or construct.

This is a broad, reproducible first pass, but it should not be described as “every paper ever published.” IEEE/ACM indexing and terminology are inconsistent, and posters or non-English work may still be missing.

**Citation-count snapshot:** The **Cited by** values below are OpenAlex `cited_by_count` values retrieved on **August 5, 2026**. They are provided as a consistent comparison, not as definitive impact scores. Counts differ across databases and will increase over time. **N/A** means OpenAlex did not return a matching indexed record.

## Search-task patterns available to borrow

1. **Conjunction search:** find one target defined by a color-shape combination among distractors sharing one feature. This is the cleanest match to the current implementation.
2. **Feature search:** find a uniquely colored or oriented target. Useful as an easy baseline or training trial, but likely too easy for the primary manipulation.
3. **Initially out-of-view search:** place the target outside the starting field of view and measure time to first orient, first inspect, and select.
4. **Wide-area treasure hunt:** hide several targets near or behind physical and virtual scene objects; measure paths, coverage, recall, and misses.
5. **Cue-reliability search:** provide valid, vague, delayed, or incorrect guidance and measure compliance, recovery, and automation bias.
6. **Peripheral-vision manipulation:** use a gaze-contingent window/scotoma or vary angular tolerance to test whether a single gaze ray undercounts inspection.
7. **Dual-task search:** add auditory monitoring or working-memory load to validate workload-sensitive behavioral and eye measures.
8. **Multiple-target search:** require several targets in one round to study resumption, memory for searched regions, and repeated hints.
9. **Physical-versus-virtual switching:** alternate target type or place virtual targets among physical distractors to quantify attention switching.
10. **Information search:** query and browse documents or data in VR. This is a different research family and is not recommended for the current thesis experiment.

## Recommended evidence stack for the current study

- Use **Warden et al. (2022)** to defend the seated rotational layout, physical-object density, gaze-supported inspection, and cue comparison.
- Use **Zhang et al. (2023)** to defend spoken hints and gaze-assisted feedback in AR search.
- Use **Binetti et al. (2021)**, **Marquardt et al. (2020)**, and **Høeg et al. (2017)** to defend audio as an out-of-view guidance channel.
- Use **David et al. (2021)** and **Stein et al. (2024)** to defend peripheral tolerance and separate eye/head measures.
- Use **Warden et al. (2023, 2024)** to defend cue-validity logging, error handling, and the risk of over-reliance.
- Use **Guo et al. (2024)** as the primary precedent for the generic/self-similar voice factor.
- Use **Kao et al. (2021)** and **Jaggy et al. (2025)** for identification and similarity-attraction mechanisms.
- Use **McGettigan et al. (2025)** and **Diel and Lewis (2024)** to justify naturalness, similarity, eeriness, familiarity, and privacy manipulation checks.

## Remaining literature gaps

- Citation-chain all 30 references in Quinn and Gabbard’s 2024 review when the full text becomes available; this is the most likely source of missed out-of-view techniques.
- Search poster-only programs for ISMAR, IEEE VR, SUI, and VRST year by year if the review must satisfy systematic-review reporting standards.
- Decide whether “search” includes wayfinding, visual analytics, medical cancellation tests, and information retrieval. They are currently retained only as adjacent categories.
- For self-similar voice, the direct evidence base remains small. The current 2 x 2 hypothesis should therefore be framed as partly confirmatory for social outcomes and exploratory for search performance and the interaction with gaze awareness.
