\begin{abstract}
Spoken guidance can help mixed-reality users locate objects outside their current field of view. Voice identity may also affect how users experience that assistance. Follow My Voice compares a participant-preferred catalog voice with a synthetic version of the participant's own voice. Target identity is withheld while gaze-contingent directions guide search across eight surrounding vertical planes. A right-controller ray and trigger provide explicit selection.

The system separates local spatial reasoning from constrained language-model decisions. A central guidance state machine combines valid gaze, controller intersections, and recent history. Jev selects eligible phrasing and speaking opportunities; local playback checks reject stale cues. Prepared ElevenLabs audio supplies both conditions. Synchronized records distinguish gaze contact, spoken acknowledgment, controller pointing, and successful selection.

The proposed within-participant study comprises one practice trial in the first assigned voice and two counterbalanced blocks of 40 measured trials. Analysis will compare active completion time while accounting for starting geometry and participant clustering. Workload, motivation, and agent experience will be assessed retrospectively after both blocks. This first pass documents the system, measurement definitions, planned analyses, and remaining validation requirements. Confirmatory participant outcomes are not yet available. The contribution at this stage is an auditable experimental platform and a design for studying personalized voice within a shared guidance mechanism.
\end{abstract}
