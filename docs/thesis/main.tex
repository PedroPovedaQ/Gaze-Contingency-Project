\begin{abstract}
A synthetic voice resembling the listener offers a form of personalization whose value for task assistance remains an empirical question. Follow My Voice investigates whether guidance delivered in a participant's self-similar voice differs from guidance delivered in a catalog voice that the participant has chosen to hear. The comparison concerns both search performance and the experience of receiving assistance, allowing perceived resemblance, preference, and usefulness to differ rather than assuming that hearing one's own synthetic voice will be beneficial.

The proposed within-participant study comprises one practice trial in the first assigned voice followed by two counterbalanced blocks of 40 measured trials. In a seated mixed-reality search task, target identity is withheld and spoken directions guide participants toward objects distributed across eight surrounding vertical planes. Both voices use the same gaze-contingent guidance policy, phrase library, and right-controller selection method. Gaze tracking and constrained agent decisions provide a shared delivery mechanism for the voice comparison; they are not separate experimental factors.

The primary analysis will compare active completion time between self-similar and preferred catalog voices while accounting for starting geometry and participant clustering. Perceived voice similarity will be checked separately from performance. Workload, motivation, and agent experience will be assessed retrospectively after both blocks, with condition-specific ratings referenced to their corresponding block; debriefing will explore reactions to hearing the self-similar voice and possible uses for it. The system records actual voice exposure and guidance delivery to help distinguish a voice-condition difference from a technical delivery problem. This first pass develops the rationale, study design, supporting apparatus, and analysis plan. Confirmatory participant outcomes are not yet available, so no performance advantage or preference for self-similar voice is claimed.
\end{abstract}
