\section{Raw Observation Records}
Gaze records will retain available source time or sequence, application time, validity, source identity, origin, direction, and sample age. Per-eye data will be labeled as such when available, and combined gaze will not be treated as independent left- and right-eye observations. Any head-direction fallback will be flagged rather than silently substituted for eye gaze in eye-derived outcomes.

Motion records will retain head and right-controller poses, tracking state, trigger state, and the time basis needed for reconstruction. Object records will retain stable identifiers, shape/color metadata, transforms, target designation, wall assignment, and layout version. Event records will identify state transitions, eligible actions, model requests and responses, cancellations, playback, interruptions, and trial outcomes. Unknown values are marked unavailable rather than filled with plausible defaults.

\section{Scoring Rules}
NASA-TLX~\cite{hart1988nasatlx} scoring retains the six raw values on the 0--10 scale. The positively phrased Performance item runs from 0 = Failure to 10 = Perfect, the reverse of the NASA form, so larger raw values indicate better perceived performance; demand, effort and frustration keep their adverse direction. The raw Performance response is preserved, and a separate workload-oriented value is calculated once as $10-r_{\mathrm{Performance}}$. The unweighted workload mean combines this reversed Performance value with the other five raw ratings, none of which is reversed, and is calculated only when all six ratings are present; higher means indicate greater workload or poorer perceived performance. Any exported normalized 0--100 field is named separately, calculated from explicit bounds, and labeled as raw or workload-oriented. Legacy data without known bounds or Performance direction remain unscored, and earlier Perfect-to-Failure responses are not reversed automatically.

IMI~\cite{ryan1982control,mcauley1989imi} scoring uses separate complete subscale means: interest/enjoyment (7), effort/importance (5), pressure/tension (5), and value/usefulness (7). On the 1--7 response scale, reverse-coded values use $8-r$. Within-subscale reverse positions are enjoyment 3 and 4, effort 2 and 5, pressure 1 and 3, and none for value. There is no overall IMI total.

The agent-perception battery selected from Guo et al.~\cite{guo2024doppelganger} retains source identifiers. Co-presence Q1--6, perceived intelligence Q13--18, and anthropomorphism Q37--42 are separate six-item means when complete. Q20 appealing, Q21 eerie, and Q22 familiar are individual ratings in their original directions. There is no 21-item or combined eeriness total. Custom voice/assistance items likewise remain individual outcomes. The five draft ARQ items~\cite{wang2020terrible} (helpfulness, relatability, appropriate behavior, likeability, willingness to interact again) are individual 1--7 ratings with no composite score.

\section{Export Validation}
Before analysis, automated checks will reject duplicate trial keys, unknown voice/block joins, negative active intervals, selection before reveal, and scores outside declared bounds, while preserving incomplete outcomes, missing contacts, absent questionnaire items, and old records with insufficient provenance. Joined tables will be checked for exactly one survey observation per participant and referenced block before survey values are repeated on trial rows. Exports will be reproducible from raw records and a versioned analysis configuration.

\section{Measurement Definitions}
\label{app:measurement-definitions}
This section gives the formal definitions summarized in Section~\ref{ch:measurement}.

\subsection{Coordinate Frames and Starting Geometry}
\label{sec:starting-geometry}
The starting angle $\theta_0$ is defined in Section~\ref{sec:theta-measures}. The sign convention is checked against Unity's world-up signed-angle convention using known left and right targets. The absolute value $|\theta_0|$ describes horizontal demand from the saved cross reference. For exactly backward targets, $-180$ and $+180$ identify the same horizontal bearing. The sign then depends on a tie convention, but the absolute magnitude is 180 degrees in either case.

The application saves this snapshot when readiness completes, before the opening cue. It records the eye origin of the valid gaze observation that completed readiness, the cross and target centers in world coordinates, that observation's sequence number and receipt time, the assigned and realized signed angles, and the target-height difference. The target is placed from the same origin, so the realized angle equals the assigned category. A difference larger than 0.01 degrees stops the session. Recordings made before the snapshot was added, including the development recordings in Section~\ref{sec:development-results}, contain only nominal signed and absolute theta fields. These fields describe wall geometry relative to the previous target wall, not the exact pre-cue angle. If a horizontal vector is degenerate, the angle is recorded as unavailable rather than zero, since zero would describe an easy starting configuration.

Each block contains three scheduled trials at each of the eight signed wall steps, which gives three trials each at $0$ and $180$ degrees and six each at $45$, $90$, and $135$ degrees. The target position is chosen to realize the assigned angle from the saved cross reference, so the target is generally offset from the center of its assigned wall. Matching the starting angle controls horizontal demand only at that instant. Total head and eye movement, vertical demand, and local clutter can still differ between trials, and movement during the opening cue and search is treated as an outcome. Both assigned and realized geometry are inspected.

The signed height difference is
\begin{equation}
\Delta h=p_y-c_y.
\end{equation}
Positive values identify targets above the cross center, and negative values identify targets below it. The reference is the cross center, not seated eye height. Each block assigns 12 targets above and 12 below the cross, balanced as evenly as possible across nominal angles. Assigned target centers lie between 10 and 45 percent of the permitted object-height range from the cross, so no target is scheduled at cross height. The snapshot records the realized height difference, which is compared with the assigned sign.

The three-dimensional distance measure relies on a different observation: the first valid tracked head position $\mathbf{h}_k$ after reveal. Its value is $d=\|\mathbf{p}-\mathbf{h}_k\|_2$, in metres, stored with the timestamp of $\mathbf{h}_k$. This distance describes geometry at an observed post-reveal head pose, so it cannot stand in for the pre-cue snapshot in recordings that lack one.

\subsection{Trial Clocks and Pause Intervals}
\label{sec:trial-clocks}
Let $t_r$ be object reveal, $t_s$ correct selection, and $\mathcal{P}$ the union of valid pause intervals. Active completion time is
\begin{equation}
T_{\mathrm{active}}=(t_s-t_r)-\mu\!\left([t_r,t_s]\cap\mathcal{P}\right),
\end{equation}
where $\mu$ denotes elapsed duration. Using the union prevents overlapping pause reasons, such as an application-focus pause that coincides with a researcher-requested pause, from being subtracted twice. The active clock remains stopped until every relevant pause reason has been resolved.

Readiness and opening speech precede reveal and are deliberately excluded from active search time. A broader interval, from opening-cue onset to selection, is also defined. It includes the opening instruction, whose duration may differ across voices. Active completion time remains the primary outcome.

A stopped or timed-out trial has an observed duration and an outcome reason but no successful completion time. A timeout records 25 seconds of active search exposure, a distinct timeout event, and an unsuccessful outcome. The software detects expiry during an update and caps the exposure clock at the deadline, while event timestamps retain the actual detection time. Earlier development recordings retain their original durations and are not retrospectively truncated to this limit. Source and receipt timestamps are likewise kept distinct, because a file that arrives late in the viewer may reflect transfer delay rather than a delay in the headset event.

Madritsch et al. distinguish overall task-completion time from active working time in an assistant-supported procedural task~\cite{madritsch2026assistance}. The active clock in Follow My Voice is defined independently of their measure. It includes ordinary waits for guidance between object reveal and correct selection and excludes only recorded pauses. Separate decision and playback timestamps help explain delivery delays without removing their task-level consequences from the primary outcome.

\subsection{Acquisition Rate, Validity, and Gaps}
\label{sec:acquisition-rate}
Eye-tracking data quality involves more than a nominal sampling frequency, and reporting guidance calls for acquisition, validity, spatial performance, and missingness to be described~\cite{dunn2024reporting,niehorster2026dataquality}. A render loop can query an eye-tracking API frequently without receiving a new hardware observation each time. The effective acquisition rate is therefore estimated from distinct source observations. If $n$ unique observations span source times $t_1$ through $t_n$, a basic rate summary is $(n-1)/(t_n-t_1)$. A mean alone can conceal bursty delivery, so the distribution of successive intervals, repeated timestamps, invalid observations, and long gaps accompanies that average.

Application timestamps describe when data were handled, whereas source timestamps describe when the tracking observation was produced, if the API exposes them reliably. Age or latency across these clocks is therefore computed only once a clock-domain mapping is established. Where unique source timing is unavailable, that limitation is reported. In that case, 120-Hz binocular delivery is not inferred from polling or headset specifications.

Spatial validation compares gaze direction with known independent targets. Angular error is computed from normalized gaze and expected target directions, with invalid observations excluded from the error distribution but retained in availability summaries. Precision and bias are assessed separately, since a stable offset and a noisy signal can produce different guidance failures even when their average angular error is similar. The validation procedure also documents target exposure, head pose, corrective lenses, and headset adjustments.

\subsection{Sequence-Level Latency}
\label{sec:sequence-latency}
For a qualified event, let $t_q$ denote the earliest supported qualification time and $t_p$ the actual software playback start of the corresponding acknowledgment. The primary software delivery delay is
\begin{equation}
L_{\mathrm{software}}=t_p-t_q.
\end{equation}
This quantity excludes acoustic latency at the participant's ear, since audio-device buffering and the physical sound path require separate measurement. Gaze and controller acknowledgments are each measured from their own qualification time, because measuring a controller acknowledgment from first gaze contact would inflate or misattribute its delay.

Repeated requests about one continuously qualified event form a single episode, so a sequence can include several decisions by the model to wait without creating several independent missed confirmations. An episode ends with playback, disqualification, trial termination, or a defined reset. The evaluation reports how those endpoints were assigned and marks incomplete evidence as unknown.

Where logs support attribution, waiting is divided into model response, explicit model deferral, scheduling, existing speech, and audio readiness. Because these intervals may overlap, they are not summed unless they are represented as a nonoverlapping partition of the critical path. The full event timeline remains the authority when aggregate attribution is ambiguous.

\subsection{Questionnaire Joins and Versioning}
\label{sec:questionnaire-joins}
Each questionnaire response refers to one voice block, but the final administration takes place after both blocks. A response therefore retains both its final-administration timing and a Block 1 or Block 2 reference. The voice identifier is joined through the saved condition order rather than inferred from block number. Practice is excluded from measured block outcomes even though it uses the first voice.

Convenience exports repeat each block-level workload score on all 24 trial rows. The analytical dataset treats that score as one participant-by-block observation and collapses duplicate joins when estimating questionnaire effects. Raw responses, score direction, scale bounds, item identifiers, instrument version, and missingness remain available.

The NASA-TLX adaptation uses 0--10 ratings, whereas older development exports used 0--100, so automated scoring relies on explicit range metadata. A separately named normalized score supports comparison while raw values are kept unchanged. Legacy responses with unknown Performance anchors are left unscored rather than assigned a direction. Complete-subscale scoring for the IMI and agent-perception measures will be verified before confirmatory analysis.
