\chapter{CONCLUSION}
\section{Self-Similar Voice as Task Assistance}
Follow My Voice examines the voice through which that assistance is received: does a synthetic voice resembling the participant change search efficiency or experience compared with a neutral voice the participant has selected? Because resemblance, liking, and usefulness need not coincide, the study examines them separately, in a task where spoken guidance progressively reveals the intended object.

The proposed within-participant comparison uses two counterbalanced 40-trial blocks after one practice trial in the first assigned voice. Active completion time is the primary outcome, with retrospective workload, motivation, agent-experience ratings, and debriefing providing complementary evidence after both blocks. Perceived-similarity ratings assess whether the synthesized voice achieves the intended resemblance, while completion-time measures evaluate search performance.

Gaze-contingent guidance supplies the shared context in which participants encounter the two voices. Local spatial reasoning, constrained Jev decisions, explicit right-trigger selection, and traceable speech delivery support a controlled and inspectable comparison. Their role is to make the voice study interpretable; this thesis does not estimate their independent effects or claim that a particular decision provider is superior.

The present contribution is a rationale and operational design for studying self-similar voice, supported by an implemented apparatus and measurement plan. Confirmatory participant results are not yet available. Exact pre-cue geometry, vertical allocation, sample-size planning, pilot acceptance criteria, and cloud-consent alignment remain requirements before the study can estimate a voice-condition contrast.

\section{Status of the Research Questions}
The primary question remains unanswered empirically: no confirmatory voice-condition contrast in active completion time is estimated here. The secondary questions likewise remain open because the final retrospective workload and motivation responses have not been collected for this report. The exploratory questions have a defined exploratory battery and debrief structure, but no participant judgments or themes are presented as findings.

For guidance delivery, the platform and data contract provide a way to inspect sensing, state, decisions, and software playback separately. The October 20 alignment audit summarized in Chapter~\ref{ch:evaluation} supports specific implementation checks. That audit does not provide a held-out guidance error rate or show that all delayed confirmations have been removed. These records describe the supporting apparatus and its current limits, while the question of whether the self-similar voice is more effective or more welcome than the neutral alternative remains for the participant study.

\section{Next Empirical Step}
The immediate work is to freeze the study configuration, close the geometry and consent gaps, validate the full wearer procedure, and finalize the primary analysis and sample-size justification before confirmatory recruitment. The resulting data must preserve counterbalanced order, actual voice exposure, observation coverage, and the final retrospective survey references. Only then can this thesis replace its results framework with estimates and revise its abstract and conclusion around observed evidence.

The intended contribution is evidence for choosing a guiding voice: whether a self-similar voice changes search efficiency in either direction, alters the experience of assistance, introduces discomfort, or leaves important differences unresolved. Those answers are bounded to the tested task and voice configurations.
