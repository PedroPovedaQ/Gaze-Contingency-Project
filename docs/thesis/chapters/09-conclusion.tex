\chapter{CONCLUSION}
\section{Self-Similar Voice as Task Assistance}
Follow My Voice asks whether a synthetic voice resembling the participant changes the efficiency or experience of assistance compared with a catalog voice the participant prefers. The distinction matters because resemblance, liking, and usefulness need not coincide. A recognizable self-similar voice could be welcome, uncomfortable, effective, or no more useful than the preferred alternative; the planned study must establish which patterns occur in this task.

The proposed within-participant comparison uses two counterbalanced 40-trial blocks after one practice trial in the first assigned voice. Active completion time is the primary outcome, with retrospective workload, motivation, agent-experience ratings, and debriefing providing complementary evidence after both blocks. Perceived similarity will be checked separately, so recognition of the cloned voice will not itself be presented as a performance benefit.

Gaze-contingent guidance supplies the shared context in which participants encounter the two voices. Local spatial reasoning, constrained Jev decisions, explicit right-trigger selection, and traceable speech delivery support a controlled and inspectable comparison. Their role is to make the voice study interpretable; this thesis does not estimate their independent effects or claim that a particular decision provider is superior.

The present contribution is a rationale and operational design for studying self-similar voice, supported by an implemented apparatus and measurement plan. Confirmatory participant results are not yet available. Exact pre-cue geometry, vertical allocation, sample-size planning, pilot acceptance criteria, and cloud-consent alignment remain requirements before the study can establish a voice-condition effect.

\section{Status of the Research Questions}
RQ1 remains unanswered empirically: no confirmatory voice-condition contrast in active completion time is estimated here. RQ2 likewise remains open because the final retrospective workload and motivation responses have not been collected for this report. RQ3 has a defined exploratory battery and debrief structure, but no participant judgments or themes are presented as findings. The literature motivates these questions; it does not supply their answers for this task.

For EQ1, the platform and data contract provide a way to inspect sensing, state, decisions, and software playback separately. The October 20 alignment audit summarized in Chapter~\ref{ch:evaluation} supports specific implementation checks. The separate October 23 working-file inspection in Appendix~\ref{app:decision-contract} documents request, validation, and memory behavior without establishing deployment. It does not establish a held-out guidance error rate or prove that all delayed confirmations have been removed. These checks qualify the supporting apparatus; they do not establish whether self-similar voice is more effective or more welcome than the preferred alternative.

\section{Next Empirical Step}
The immediate work is to freeze the study configuration, close the geometry and consent gaps, validate the full wearer procedure, and finalize the primary analysis and sample-size justification before confirmatory recruitment. The resulting data must preserve counterbalanced order, actual voice exposure, observation coverage, and the final retrospective survey references. Only then can this thesis replace its results framework with estimates and revise its abstract and conclusion around observed evidence.

The completed thesis should ultimately explain what participants gained, disliked, or found unchanged when guidance used a voice resembling their own. Its conclusions will be bounded to the tested task and voice configurations, with performance and experience reported separately. The value of the supporting technology is that it makes that empirical question possible to answer.
