\chapter{CONCLUSION}
\section{Self-Similar Voice as Task Assistance}
Follow My Voice examines the voice through which task assistance is delivered: does a synthetic voice resembling the participant change search efficiency or experience compared with a neutral voice the participant has selected? Because resemblance, liking, and usefulness need not coincide, the study examines them separately, in a task where spoken guidance progressively reveals the intended object.

The proposed within-participant comparison uses two counterbalanced 40-trial blocks after one practice trial in the first assigned voice. Active completion time is the primary outcome, with retrospective workload, motivation, agent-experience ratings, and debriefing providing complementary evidence after both blocks. Perceived-similarity ratings assess whether the synthesized voice achieves the intended resemblance, while completion-time measures evaluate search performance.

Gaze-contingent guidance supplies the shared context in which participants encounter the two voices. Local spatial reasoning, constrained Jev decisions, explicit right-trigger selection, and traceable speech delivery support a controlled and inspectable comparison. They are intended to make the voice comparison interpretable; this thesis does not estimate their independent effects or compare decision providers.

This thesis contributes a rationale and study design for examining self-similar voice, together with an implemented apparatus and measurement plan. Confirmatory participant results are not yet available. Sample-size planning, pilot acceptance criteria, and alignment of the consent documents with cloud processing must be completed before the study can estimate a voice-condition contrast.

\section{Status of the Research Questions}
The primary question has not yet been answered empirically, as no confirmatory voice-condition contrast in active completion time is estimated here. The secondary questions likewise remain open because the final retrospective workload and motivation responses have not yet been collected. For the exploratory questions, the questionnaire battery and debrief structure are defined, but no participant judgments or themes are reported as findings.

For guidance delivery, the platform and its data dictionary allow sensing, state, decisions, and software playback to be inspected separately. The October 20, 2026 verification run summarized in Chapter~\ref{ch:evaluation} documents specific software checks but provides neither a held-out guidance error rate nor evidence that all delayed confirmations have been eliminated. These records describe the supporting apparatus and its current limits; whether the self-similar voice is more effective or more welcome than the neutral alternative remains a question for the participant study.

\section{Next Empirical Step}
Before confirmatory recruitment, the study configuration will be fixed, the consent documents reconciled with the services actually used, the full procedure validated with wearers, and the primary analysis and sample-size justification finalized. The resulting data will record counterbalanced order, actual voice exposure, observation coverage, and the block referenced by each retrospective survey set. Only then can the results framework be replaced with estimates and the abstract and conclusion revised around observed evidence.

The intended contribution is evidence for choosing a guiding voice: whether a self-similar voice changes search efficiency in either direction, alters the experience of assistance, introduces discomfort, or leaves important differences unresolved. Those answers will be limited to the tested task and voice configurations.
