\subsection{Scoring}
\label{sec:scoring}
\subsubsection{Task Performance}
\paragraph{Active completion time.} The primary outcome is elapsed active-search time in seconds from object reveal to the correct right-trigger selection, excluding the union of recorded pause intervals overlapping that period. Section~\ref{ch:measurement} specifies the clock equation and handling of overlapping pauses. Lower values indicate faster completion. Readiness, opening guidance, post-selection feedback, questionnaires and between-trial breaks do not contribute to this outcome. An unfinished trial retains its observed exposure and outcome but has no successful completion time. The full interval from opening-cue onset to selection is also defined to describe the complete instructed task duration.

\paragraph{Target contact and selection errors.} Time to first target gaze is the active-clock time of the first valid, recorded gaze-ray intersection with the target, and time to first controller contact uses the first tracked right-controller ray--target intersection. Last target-gaze contact time denotes the last observed valid target intersection during the trial, not a fixation duration or a spoken confirmation. These event times remain distinct from trigger selection and from the larger gaze-vicinity region used by the guidance system. Incorrect selections are counted as trigger presses that select non-target objects. If no valid target contact is observed, its latency is missing rather than zero. All contact times retain their source and sampling resolution.

\subsubsection{Starting Geometry and Search Behavior}
\paragraph{Starting angle, height and distance.} The pre-cue snapshot saves a reference origin (the eye origin at readiness completion), readiness-cross position and target position. Starting theta ($\theta_0$) is the signed angle about world up between the horizontal origin-to-cross and origin-to-target vectors. Its magnitude $|\theta_0|$ quantifies horizontal turn demand, and its sign preserves direction. Degenerate horizontal vectors are marked unavailable. The primary analysis adjusts for $|\theta_0|$ rather than dividing completion time by degrees. Nominal wall-angle assignment, exact pre-cue theta and post-reveal head-to-target error are separate variables. Recordings made before the snapshot was added carry only nominal theta and therefore lack $\theta_0$.

Signed target-height difference is $\Delta h=y_{\mathrm{target}}-y_{\mathrm{cross}}$, in metres; positive values indicate above-cross targets. Euclidean target distance is the three-dimensional distance between target center and the first valid tracked head position after reveal, with that observation's timestamp retained. All positions used in a calculation share a recorded coordinate frame.

\paragraph{Head movement and zone coverage.} Gross head translation sums distances between adjacent valid head positions, in metres, and gross head rotation sums shortest quaternion angular displacements between adjacent valid orientations, in degrees. Fixed-axis component totals are retained separately and labeled by their calculation rather than as Euler yaw, pitch and roll. Invalid intervals, pauses and excessive sample gaps break accumulation, and the analysis does not interpolate across them. Covered time and gap counts accompany totals. These measures describe head movement, not independently tracked chair rotation.

Distinct walls visited, wall transitions and revisits summarize search coverage using stable valid gaze observations and a versioned zone-assignment rule. Transitions across tracking gaps are not inferred.

\paragraph{Instructions and guidance timing.} The delivered active-search assistance total counts playback starts for directional guidance, gaze/pointing acknowledgments, and selection reminders, including delivered repeats. These categories are also reported separately, as specified in Section~\ref{ch:measurement}. Requested repeats, delivered repeats and interrupted utterances are retained separately. Model requests, prefetched audio and success speech are excluded from delivered search instructions. Opening cues and success feedback retain separate event labels. Timing analysis distinguishes evidence qualification, decision-request latency, model deferral, scheduling delay and audio onset. Gaze confirmations and controller confirmations are evaluated separately against their corresponding gates. The controller gate includes both its hold and the required nearby valid gaze.

\subsubsection{Subjective Measures}
\paragraph{Workload.} Workload is measured with an adaptation of \href{https://doi.org/10.1016/S0166-4115(08)62386-9}{Hart and Staveland's NASA-TLX}~\cite{hart1988nasatlx}. Six dimensions---mental, physical and temporal demand, performance, effort and frustration---are rated separately for each voice block during the final survey, after both blocks. The study uses a documented 0--10 adaptation without pairwise weighting. Demand, effort and frustration run from 0 = Very low to 10 = Very high. The Performance prompt asks how successful the participant was and runs from 0 = Failure to 10 = Perfect. This favorable-direction response is a deliberate local adaptation of the opposite orientation in the \href{https://human-factors.arc.nasa.gov/groups/TLX/downloads/TLXScale.pdf}{NASA rating form}. All six raw responses are retained; higher raw Performance values indicate better perceived performance. Before calculating the workload mean, only Performance is reverse-scored once as $10-r_{\mathrm{Performance}}$. The unweighted mean uses the other five raw ratings and this reversed Performance value, and is calculated only when all six responses are present. Higher mean scores therefore indicate greater workload or poorer perceived performance.

\paragraph{Motivation and activity experience.} The \href{https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/intrinsic-motivation-inventory/}{Intrinsic Motivation Inventory}~\cite{ryan1982control,mcauley1989imi} contributes interest/enjoyment (7 items), effort/importance (5), pressure/tension (5), and value/usefulness (7). Responses range from 1 = Not at all true to 7 = Very true. Published reverse keys are applied before averaging each complete subscale. Interest/enjoyment represents intrinsic motivation, and the four subscales are not combined into an overall IMI score. Activity wording refers to the completed search block, with task-specific adaptations documented.

\paragraph{Agent perception.} Twenty-one items from \href{https://doi.org/10.1145/3651288}{Guo et al. (2024), Appendix A, Table A1}~\cite{guo2024doppelganger} assess co-presence (Q1--6), perceived intelligence (Q13--18), appealing/eerie/familiar impressions (Q20--22), and perceived anthropomorphism (Q37--42). ``Virtual character'' is adapted to ``voice agent.'' Responses range from 1 = Very strongly disagree to 7 = Very strongly agree. The three six-item constructs have separate complete-scale means. Q20--22 are analyzed individually rather than combined into a multi-item eeriness index of the kind refined in uncanny-valley research~\cite{homacdorman2017eeriness}.

\paragraph{Agent rating.} Five draft items adapt the Agent Rating Questionnaire (ARQ)~\cite{wang2020terrible}, as used by Venkatakrishnan et al. for a task-guiding agent in VR~\cite{venkatakrishnan2026clarifying}, with one item per dimension: helpfulness, relatability, appropriate behavior, likeability, and willingness to interact again. Responses range from 1 = Very strongly disagree to 7 = Very strongly agree. The items are analyzed individually as exploratory measures of agent perception.

\paragraph{Voice experience and reliance.} Twelve custom 1--7 agreement items assess voice similarity, responsiveness, helpfulness, distraction, familiarity, trust, comfort, uncanniness, reliance, naturalness, willingness to follow and motivation to act without guidance. Voice similarity serves as a manipulation check. Voice liking and retrospective percentage of guidance use each use 0--100 responses with separate unable-to-answer options. These 14 numeric items are analyzed individually, as they do not form a validated scale. Liking during voice audition remains separate from condition-specific liking collected at the end.

\paragraph{Debrief.} Open debrief questions explore perceived performance, guidance experience, own-voice comfort, possible use cases and voice preference. Broad experience questions precede specific eeriness or inner-dialogue probes.

In the final survey, each item is answered side by side for the Selected Voice and the Self-Similar Voice. Each voice column contains 70 numeric responses (140 in total): six workload ratings, 24 IMI items, 21 Guo items, five ARQ items, and 14 custom ratings. Technical checks and optional text are additional. Questionnaire observations have participant-by-block scope even when repeated across the 40 trial summaries of a block, and missing answers remain missing rather than zero.

\subsection{Validating the Measures}
\label{sec:measure-validation}
A measure can fail in two different ways. It can be computed incorrectly, for example by counting pause time as search time or treating a missing gaze sample as a valid one. Or it can be computed exactly as defined and still not reflect the construct it is meant to capture, as when a ray intersection is read as a fixation or a pupil signal driven by luminance is read as workload. The first is a question of verification; the second is a question of validation. Follow My Voice addresses both at four levels: automated tests with known answers, reconstruction of each trial from its raw records, validation of the eye tracker for each participant, and a dedicated validation session for the eye-event and pupil measures. \subsubsection{Computational Verification}
Every derived measure is computed by analysis code that is exercised by automated tests on inputs whose correct output is known in advance. The tests encode the definitions in Section~\ref{ch:measurement} as checkable cases.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Fixations, saccades and head turns.} Synthetic gaze streams containing two known fixations must yield exactly those fixations. A known saccade must be detected, while a head turn must be excluded.
\item \textbf{Trial clocks.} Recorded pause intervals must be removed from the active-search clock. A 25-second timeout must remain a timeout rather than a selection. A technical failure must not be counted as a missed target.
\item \textbf{Missing data.} Unknown tracking must never become valid gaze. Missing values must remain missing rather than becoming zero, while an observed zero is retained.
\item \textbf{Inconsistent inputs.} The export refuses inconsistent inputs, such as conflicting target identities, two recordings for one trial, or a regressing clock, rather than producing a value from them.
\item \textbf{Questionnaire scoring.} NASA-TLX scoring must reject unknown scale directions and unsupported ranges instead of guessing.
\item \textbf{Session schedule.} Schedule checks confirm the trial allocation for both voice orders.
\end{itemize}
These tests establish that the code implements its definitions. They do not show that the definitions measure what they are intended to measure.

\subsubsection{Reconstruction Checks}
Trial summaries are derived from, and stored separately from, the frame-level gaze, motion, and event records (Figure~\ref{fig:data-provenance}). Every exported value can therefore be traced to the observations it came from. Trial Review replays a trial on a common timeline with the reconstructed scene, the recorded gaze and controller rays, guidance transitions, model decisions, and speech playback (Section~\ref{sec:trial-review}). A reviewer can step to the frame at which the export places a target contact, selection, or speech onset and confirm that the event occurred there. Instruction counts come from recorded playback starts rather than from requested cues, so a cue that was requested but never played does not count as delivered guidance. Before confirmatory recruitment, pilot sessions confirm that trial events can be reconstructed from the saved data for sessions whose course is directly observed (Section~\ref{sec:pilot}).

\subsubsection{Eye-Tracker Validation for Each Participant}
Gaze-derived measures are only as accurate as the gaze signal. After the headset is fitted, vendor calibration is followed by an independent spatial check: the participant looks at targets at known positions, and the angular error between the recorded gaze direction and the true target direction is computed. Following eye-tracking reporting guidance~\cite{dunn2024reporting,niehorster2026dataquality}, accuracy (the average offset) and precision (the sample-to-sample spread) are reported separately. A stable offset and a noisy signal produce different guidance failures even when their mean errors are similar. Invalid observations are excluded from the error distribution but counted in availability summaries. If an offset correction is applied, the targets used to fit it are kept separate from those used to judge it.

The effective acquisition rate is estimated from distinct source observations rather than from rendered frames (Section~\ref{ch:measurement}). Because the headset does not currently expose native capture timestamps, a 120-Hz rate is not assumed. Tracking validity, data coverage, and gaps accompany every gaze-derived measure. Ray intersections and vicinity hits are reported as interaction events, not fixations.

\subsubsection{Eye Validation Session}
Fixation, saccade, and pupil measures require evidence that a test with synthetic data cannot provide. They are therefore validated with a separate in-headset session controlled from Trial Review, in which every stimulus has a known position, timing, or difficulty. The session contains seven groups of exercises:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Spatial accuracy.} Twenty-seven fixation dots, at three distances (1.2, 2.4, and 3.2~m), three horizontal angles ($-15^\circ$, $0^\circ$, $+15^\circ$), and three vertical angles ($-10^\circ$, $0^\circ$, $+10^\circ$), measure accuracy and precision across the region in which targets appear.
\item \textbf{Fixations and saccades.} A 20-second steady fixation and 24 cued jumps between eight known positions provide fixation periods and gaze shifts with known onset and amplitude. These are compared with the dispersion-based fixation detector and the velocity-based saccade detector~\cite{salvucci2000fixations}. A dot jump is a cue rather than a measured saccade, so detector output is also compared with independent annotation of the recorded traces, and the detector thresholds are evaluated on data held out from threshold selection.
\item \textbf{Controls for false events.} Cued blinks, a slow head turn while the eyes hold a world-fixed dot, and smooth pursuit of a moving dot test whether blinks, head motion, or pursuit are mistaken for fixations or saccades.
\item \textbf{Pupil confounds.} Background luminance changes and steady gaze at different horizontal angles measure how much pupil size, and therefore IPA, changes for reasons unrelated to workload.
\item \textbf{Workload contrast.} Four counterbalanced blocks of easy and hard mental arithmetic, with recorded answers and difficulty ratings, provide a manipulation with known difficulty. IPA~\cite{duchowski2018ipa} is treated as a workload indicator for this apparatus only if it separates hard from easy arithmetic by more than the variation produced by the luminance and gaze-angle controls.
\item \textbf{Practice search.} Short practice searches in a separate environment check selection behavior.
\item \textbf{Drift check.} Five final dots measure drift without fitting a new correction.
\end{itemize}

The session is implemented and was first run with a wearer on October 27, 2026, when a development participant completed the five drift targets with in-headset confirmation. The spatial, saccade, control, and arithmetic exercises have not yet been completed with a wearer. The session report therefore records the measurements as not validated. Acceptance criteria for the detectors and for IPA will be fixed from pilot data before confirmatory analysis. Until those criteria are met, fixation counts, saccade rates, and IPA are reported as exploratory, and final fixation duration on the target remains unavailable (Section~\ref{ch:measurement}).

\subsubsection{Questionnaire Measures}
NASA-TLX~\cite{hart1988nasatlx} and the published instruments in Table~\ref{tab:battery} are used with their established item structure. The ARQ items are taken from Venkatakrishnan et al.~\cite{venkatakrishnan2026clarifying}. Wording adapted from an embodied agent to a voice agent is documented in Appendix~\ref{app:instruments}. Scale metadata distinguishes the present 0--10 workload form from earlier exports that used another range, so responses are never rescaled implicitly. The custom voice and assistance items have no prior validation. They are therefore analyzed as individual items, with no combined score, and perceived voice similarity serves as a manipulation check rather than an outcome. Pilot sessions measure survey comprehension and completion time before recruitment.
