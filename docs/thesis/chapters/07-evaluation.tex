\chapter{VOICE-COMPARISON RESULTS FRAMEWORK AND STUDY READINESS}
\label{ch:evaluation}
The completed results chapter will follow the same question introduced at the start of the thesis: what changes when guidance sounds like the participant rather than a voice they prefer? It will establish the delivered comparison, report search efficiency, and then examine workload, motivation, and exploratory experience. No confirmatory participant outcomes are available in this draft. The reporting framework below keeps those unanswered questions visible and separates them from the engineering evidence available for the apparatus.

\section{Participant Results to Be Added}
The completed empirical chapter will first report participant flow, exclusions, incomplete sessions, and data coverage by condition and order. It will then describe realized starting geometry and the similarity manipulation check before presenting the primary voice contrast. This order allows readers to assess whether the intended comparison was delivered and measured.

The primary result will include the fitted time contrast, its uncertainty, the analyzed participant and trial counts, and model diagnostics. Secondary sections will then report workload and IMI subscales at participant-by-block level, followed by exploratory sections on agent perception, voice experience, guidance behavior, and debrief themes. Tables will identify missing observations rather than silently change denominators.

\subsection{Results Organized by Research Question}
The completed results chapter will report the voice-similarity manipulation check before the outcome contrasts. RQ1 will use the marginal time contrast standardized over a common starting-difficulty distribution, as defined in Chapter~\ref{ch:analysis}; this is not a standardized effect-size label. Its uncertainty, eligible participant/trial counts, and model diagnostics will accompany the estimate, using the analysis plan finalized before confirmatory recruitment. RQ2 will report the paired workload and separate IMI outcomes with multiplicity families to be finalized in the analysis plan. RQ3 will report exploratory agent-perception and custom ratings alongside debrief themes and valid search measures. A missing or inconclusive contrast will be identified as such rather than omitted.

EQ1 will be reported separately through sequence coverage, qualification-to-playback delay, provider reliability, and categorized faults. A clean engineering test does not answer a voice-effect research question. Conversely, a voice-condition difference does not by itself show that the guidance mechanism was correct. This separation follows the evidence map in Table~\ref{tab:rq-map} and prevents a successful build or a favorable judge rating from becoming an empirical conclusion.

\section{Evidence Available for This Draft}
This chapter separates existing engineering records from participant results that have not yet been collected. The October 20 alignment audit documents software checks, analysis tests, and an Android build/install/launch. These records support specific implementation claims, but they do not establish a treatment effect, a naturalness rating, eye-tracker accuracy, or acoustic response latency.

The audit reports passing schedule and voice-isolation checks, including one practice trial in the first assigned voice, two measured blocks, and nominal-angle quotas, and it records 38 trial-export tests and seven workload scoring/plot tests. These counts describe the audit's test run rather than a newly executed suite for this manuscript. The verification record should therefore accompany any later thesis statement so that the tested revision remains identifiable.

\section{Guidance Quality Supporting the Voice Contrast}
The completed report will describe gaze availability, actual speech exposure, delayed acknowledgments, interruptions, and unhelpful cues by voice condition and session. This account will help identify whether a condition was affected by a technical delivery problem. It will not automatically adjust away behavior-dependent hint counts or waiting time: these can occur during the task and are not interchangeable with baseline difficulty, as discussed in Chapter~\ref{ch:analysis}.

Gaze acknowledgment, controller acknowledgment, and correct trigger selection remain separate events. Their sequence records can establish what feedback was delivered, while participant ratings and debriefing address how that feedback was experienced. An advisory model judge cannot supply the latter evidence. Detailed policy pilots, episode definitions, failure categories, and the investigation of two-second delays are retained in Appendix~\ref{app:guidance-support}; their purpose is to qualify the apparatus before interpreting a self-similar voice effect.
