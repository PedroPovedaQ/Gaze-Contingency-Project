\chapter{LITERATURE REVIEW}
\label{ch:literature}
\section{Visual Search and Attention Guidance in Extended Reality}
Search in a surrounding display differs from search on a single front-facing screen because relevant objects may lie outside the visible field. Turning changes what can be inspected, while eye movements refine attention within the visible region. Work on eye and head movements across an extended field of view is therefore relevant to the task geometry~\cite{stein2024eyehead}. In the present study, this motivates recording both head motion and gaze rather than treating one as a complete substitute for the other.

The project's bibliography includes several approaches to directing attention in immersive environments. Grogorick et al. address subtle gaze guidance, while HiveFive concerns immersion-preserving attention guidance~\cite{grogorick2017subtle,lange2020hivefive}. Ariza and colleagues investigate vibrotactile assistance and proximity-based multimodal feedback for selection~\cite{ariza2017vibrotactile,ariza2018proximity}. These works establish relevant design alternatives; they do not imply that all forms of guidance are equivalent. A cue that changes visual salience, a vibration that indicates proximity, and a spoken spatial instruction impose different perceptual and interpretive demands.

Binetti et al. examine visual and auditory cues for locating out-of-view objects in head-mounted augmented reality~\cite{binetti2021outofview}. Warden et al. focus on target cue type and location in augmented-reality search~\cite{warden2022visual}. Together, these topics make clear why target visibility and cue availability belong in the method description. Follow My Voice does not present an opening color-and-shape target description. Instead, the direction sequence gradually narrows the participant's search.

This hidden-target design creates an important boundary. A participant who already knows the target can continue searching without further assistance. Here, the participant must obtain enough spatial information to infer which object is intended. The voice may consequently be heard more often and may be more consequential than in a task where it supplements an already known target. That is a reason to describe the task precisely, not a reason to assume stronger voice effects in advance.

\section{Gaze-Contingent Auditory Guidance}
Losing et al. connect gaze-contingent auditory feedback with visual-search guidance~\cite{losing2014guiding}. Two-Step Gaze Guidance provides another relevant precedent for staged guidance~\cite{kwok2022twostep}. The present system adopts a coarse-to-fine organization: orientation toward the correct wall precedes within-wall horizontal and vertical refinement. This is a design adaptation. Its particular geometry, phrases, timers, and confirmation regions require validation in the present apparatus.

Zhang et al. examine visual and audio hints alongside gaze-assisted post-task feedback in augmented-reality visual search~\cite{zhang2023seehear}. That combination is useful for distinguishing guidance during a task from feedback after it. Follow My Voice similarly separates navigation phrases, target acknowledgments, and success feedback in its logs. A success chime after selection cannot explain an earlier change in search direction in the same trial, although it may influence subsequent experience or motivation.

In Zhang et al.'s book-searching study, 96 participants were divided between two 48-participant sub-studies with and without gaze-assisted post-task feedback. The authors reported that the combined visual/audio benefit was conditional on task feedback~\cite{zhang2023seehear}. This is relevant because assistance modality and feedback timing interact with task design. Their target book was specified to the user; the present task withholds identity and keeps the guidance mechanism common across voices. Their sample size is not a power justification for this experiment.

The current study does not include an unguided or gaze-unaware control. Both voice conditions receive the same gaze-contingent policy. Earlier project documents that considered a factorial combination of voice and gaze contingency are historical, and their interpretation cannot be transferred to this two-condition design. Any observed difference will concern the assigned voice experience within the shared assistance system.

\section{Voice as a Social and Personalized Interface}
Research on machine-generated speech has considered personality, source attribution, and social presence~\cite{nass1993voices,nass2001personality,lee2005socialpresence}. These perspectives motivate treating voice as more than a neutral transport for words. They do not establish that every listener will form the same impression, or that a social impression necessarily improves task performance.

For this project, personalization has two meanings. Catalog personalization allows the participant to choose among existing voices. Self-similar personalization uses recorded speech to create a synthetic voice resembling that participant. Both forms may produce familiarity or liking, but they operate through different routes. Preference is selected directly in the catalog condition, whereas perceived resemblance must be checked in the self-similar condition. The manipulation is therefore not adequately described as personalized versus generic.

The bibliography also includes work on voice-assistant personality customization and similarity~\cite{snyder2023onevoice}. Personality similarity is conceptually different from acoustic self-resemblance. A listener can prefer a speaking style without hearing it as their own voice, and a close acoustic resemblance can be experienced as uncomfortable. The planned manipulation check asks about perceived voice similarity, while the experience battery separately asks about liking, naturalness, familiarity, and uncanniness.

\section{Self-Similar Voice and Embodied Collaboration}
Kao et al. investigate self-similar avatar voices in educational games~\cite{kao2021selfsimilar}. Guo et al. examine self-similar appearance and voice in a jigsaw-puzzle co-solving task with a virtual character~\cite{guo2024doppelganger}. These studies are closely related to the manipulation, but the surrounding interactions differ. An avatar can supply visual identity cues, educational games can include learning outcomes, and a collaborator can have a visible body. Follow My Voice uses a voice agent to guide spatial search without a visible agent body.

Guo et al. reported that self-similar voices increased likability and believability ratings while also increasing eeriness. Appearance self-similarity affected co-presence and perceived intelligence, and matched appearance/voice similarity influenced believability~\cite{guo2024doppelganger}. These results argue against treating resemblance as uniformly positive. They motivate retaining both favorable and unfavorable experience measures here. They do not predict faster object search, because their evidence concerns a different task and embodied-character configuration.

The distinction between embodiment of the participant and embodiment of the agent is important. Participants wear a headset and use a tracked controller, so the interaction involves physical sensing and action. The guiding agent, however, is not represented by a co-present animated character. Measures adapted from an embodied collaborator must therefore be described as adaptations. Their use does not establish that the same factor structure or interpretation holds for a disembodied voice.

Self-voice applications in daily reminders and emotional self-voice interventions extend the range of contexts represented in the bibliography~\cite{kim2024selfvoice,fang2025emotional}. They motivate asking whether participants can imagine uses for their own synthetic voice. They do not establish that navigation guidance will be more effective or that participants will experience it as inner speech. The debrief consequently begins with open questions before introducing probes about eeriness, self-talk, and possible applications.

\section{Naturalness, Familiarity, and Uncanniness}
Voice resemblance is not the only acoustic difference that listeners may notice. Synthesis can change articulation, prosody, loudness, duration, or perceived naturalness. Research on vocal uncanniness provides a reason to keep these qualities visible rather than subsume them under similarity~\cite{diel2024uncanny}. A clone that is recognizable but difficult to understand is not equivalent to a recognizable and intelligible clone.

The experiment will use the same semantic library and provider for both conditions. That is an implementation control, not proof of equal acoustics. Prepared clips can differ in duration and pronunciation even when their text matches. Pilot checks will inspect intelligibility and comfortable playback levels across catalog and cloned voices. Actual provider, model, voice, and library identifiers will identify the configuration a participant heard.

Custom experience items and the appealing, eerie, and familiar items selected from Guo et al. will remain separate. Combining them would imply a measurement model not established for this study. A participant may report a voice as both familiar and eerie. Preserving the individual directions allows that response to remain interpretable.

\section{Context and Short-Term Memory in XR Agents}
EmBARDiment is a relevant IEEE VR example of an embodied agent using contextual information in XR~\cite{bovo2025embardiment}. The project draws on its context-management motivation while implementing its own bounded history. The history here records recent spatial progress, actual speech, and guidance transitions. It does not provide long-term autobiographical memory or an inferred model of the participant's mental state.

Memory-Augmented AR Agents and GenLARP represent additional directions in the bibliography~\cite{choi2025memory,yu2025genlarp}. The former is a conceptual framework and the latter a short system description; neither supplies a validated timing threshold for this application. User-aware virtual agents and proactive AR assistance also offer context for designing state-dependent behavior~\cite{gunawardhana2024useraware,li2025satori}. These works position a design problem, rather than prove that this implementation's memory mechanism improves performance.

Short-term history is operationally useful when the same current geometry can have different meanings. A target to the left may be one the participant has not yet approached, or one they have just passed while turning right. A recently delivered cue can make another identical cue unnecessary. History distinguishes these cases only if it records reliable observations and actual playback. Holding this mechanism constant across voices means the study cannot estimate its independent effect.

\section{Eye-Tracking Quality and Interpretation}
Eye-tracking quality involves more than a nominal sampling frequency. The reporting guidance of Dunn et al. and the data-quality discussion of Niehorster et al. motivate reporting acquisition, validity, spatial performance, and missingness~\cite{dunn2024reporting,niehorster2026dataquality}. The system must establish how many distinct source observations arrive, how old they are when used, and how error varies across the search space.

Validation work on the Vive Pro Eye and tools such as GazeMetrics are methodological resources~\cite{schuetz2022vive,sipatchin2021usability,adhanom2020gazemetrics}. They are not substitutes for validation on the HTC Vive Focus Vision. Device model, fit, calibration, coordinate conversion, and task geometry can differ. The protocol therefore requires independent targets across heights, boundaries, distances, and head orientations. Validation targets must be separate from targets used to estimate an offset correction.

Drewes et al. compare gaze during locomotion in virtual reality and the real world~\cite{drewes2021locomotion}. This study is seated and uses head and chair rotation rather than free walking, so locomotion findings cannot be imported as direct estimates of its gaze behavior. Head motion will be logged because it participates in search, while claims about eye events remain bounded by the available measurements.

Salvucci and Goldberg discuss identifying fixations and saccades from eye-tracking protocols~\cite{salvucci2000fixations}. An interaction region around an object serves a different purpose. The 100-ms vicinity qualification in this system is an interface acknowledgment rule, not a validated fixation classifier. The thesis will report gaze contacts and qualified vicinity events under their own names.

\section{Workload, Motivation, and Agent Perception}
Zagermann et al. discuss eye tracking as a means of investigating cognitive load in visual computing~\cite{zagermann2016workload}. That motivates considering gaze-derived measures, but does not license interpreting an unvalidated ray-hit count as workload. The current confirmatory plan uses subjective workload dimensions and task performance. Pupil, fixation, and saccade analyses remain contingent on separate acquisition and event-validation work.

NASA-TLX supplies six workload dimensions~\cite{hart1988nasatlx}. The project's 0--10 response range and unweighted complete mean are documented adaptations. The Intrinsic Motivation Inventory distinguishes interest/enjoyment from related subscales such as effort, pressure, and value~\cite{ryan1982control,mcauley1989imi,csdtIMI}. Separate subscale reporting preserves that distinction. The instrument appendix specifies current wording and reverse keys so scoring can be reproduced.

Agent perception measures are drawn through Guo et al., with attribution to their originating measures~\cite{guo2024doppelganger,biocca2001networked,moussawi2019perception}. Co-presence, perceived intelligence, and anthropomorphism concern participant attributions. A high rating does not establish sentience or decision correctness. Conversely, deterministic validity checks do not establish that a participant found the agent natural. The design needs both kinds of evidence while keeping their meanings separate.

The resulting contribution is a particular combination of task and comparison. The agent knows an initially unnamed target, the participant receives adaptive spatial speech, and explicit right-trigger selection remains separate from gaze. The empirical manipulation changes the voice configuration while retaining that assistance mechanism. This combination defines the scope of RQ1--RQ3; it does not imply that voice familiarity, memory, and gaze contingency have been independently manipulated.

\section{Synthesis and Research Gap}
The selected literature supports a study at the intersection of spatial assistance and voice identity. Gaze-contingent guidance provides a mechanism for adapting directions to search. Self-similar voice work motivates a personalized voice comparison. Eye-tracking and questionnaire methods establish requirements for measuring that comparison without overstating what the signals mean.

The specific gap addressed here is preferred versus self-similar voice delivery in an initially unnamed, surrounding object-search task under a common adaptive policy. The thesis does not claim that no related system exists. Instead, it makes the comparison operational: the task, shared mechanism, voice assignment, outcome boundaries, and measurement limitations are specified together. The answer will depend on the completed study rather than on an assumption that familiar voices are necessarily better.
