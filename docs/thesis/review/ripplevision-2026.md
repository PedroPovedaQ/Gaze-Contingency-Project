## What was verified

Crossref and PubMed agree on authors, title, DOI, volume, issue, and final page range. Use 3884–3893 rather than early-access 1–10. The abstract describes a gaze-dependent visual technique with brightness ripples between the point of interest and gaze location, using inverted polarity between the eyes. It reports calibration followed by a comparison with four techniques; the details and numerical outcomes have not been inspected and are not imported into the thesis.

IEEE returned a JavaScript verification page rather than the article or PDF. Checked author and university pages linked back to IEEE; no accessible full text was located. An OpenAlex lookup reported closed access and no repository full text. This is an access limitation, not evidence that no author manuscript exists.

| Future modality comparison | Potential visual comparator if a separate future study contrasts visual and spoken guidance. | Protocol proposal only; do not add a treatment to the current voice study. |
| Future apparatus review | Inspect calibration method and robustness to gaze error once full text is obtained. | Open review item; no threshold transfers to Focus Vision. |

The current manipulation remains self-similar versus participant-preferred catalog voice under a shared guidance policy. RippleVision does not test that manipulation and cannot support claims about self-voice, agent intelligence, Jev, voice naturalness, or our hidden-target task's effectiveness. Its point-of-interest guidance does not, by itself, establish that its participants were initially unaware of target identity.
