  Manuscript Submission Checklist – Formatting & Accessibility
The College of Graduate Studies may refuse manuscripts for format review or final submission if
they do not comply with the following formatting and digital accessibility requirements, are
otherwise unsuitable for publication, and/or submitted outside of established deadlines.

☐ Is your thesis or dissertation at least 90% complete? (e.g., all chapters drafted, figures/tables
included, appendixes and references substantially complete)

☐ Has all of your thesis and dissertation content been copied into the official Word or LaTeX
template?

☐ Have you converted your Word or LaTeX document to a bookmarked PDF?

☐ Have you added a document title? (in Word, see File > Info > Properties > Add your thesis or
dissertation title to the title field)

☐ Are document sections in the correct order?

☐ Is the title page formatted correctly? (fill in all template fields correctly on this page)

☐ Do all entries in table of contents, list of figures, and/or list of tables link properly?

☐ Do page numbers follow requirements?

☐ Are margins correct with no content appearing outside them?

☐ Have Heading styles been applied consistently to all major headings and subheadings?

☐ Has alternative text been applied to all images in the document (including Appendixes
content)?

☐ Have header rows been identified in all tables and have you ensured there are no split or
merged cells?

☐ Have figure and table captions been properly added and positioned correctly?

☐ Have you run an Accessibility Check to verify there are no issues? (in Word or using Adobe
Acrobat Pro; see next page)
       Checking for Accessibility in Your Thesis or Dissertation
The accessibility checker in Word will catch many issues, but it is not as robust as the accessibility
checker in Adobe Pro. Adobe Pro is available in the UCF Library as well as other labs on campus.

If you do not have access to run an accessibility check in Adobe Pro, we encourage you to schedule
a Format Help appointment via Calendy to have someone run it for you.

If you have questions regarding the results of the accessibility check, reach out to the Digital
Accessibility Lab.


In Word
   •   Navigate to the Review tab in your document
   •   Click on Check Accessibility
   •   The Accessibility Assistant panel will open on the right side of the document
   •   Beside each item listed, look for either a blue check mark indicating that everything looks
       good, or a number indicating correction(s) to be made
   •   If there is a number, click on it to resolve the issue. For example, if alt text is needed,
       clicking on Missing Alt Text will take you to the area where you can add alt text to the figure


In Adobe Pro
   •   Open the Tools panel
   •   Select Accessibility
   •   In the new menu that opens, select Accessibility Check
   •   The Accessibility Checker Options window will open. Make sure the following options are
       selected:
            o Page Range – all pages in the document
            o Category – Document
            o Accessibility permission flag is set
            o Document is not image-only PDF
            o Document is tagged PDF
            o Text language is specified
            o Document title is showing in title bar
            o Bookmarks are present in large documents
   •   Click Start Checking
   •   The Accessibility Checker panel will appear with issues in bold
   •   Click on an issue for more information
