## Claim evidence

- **Kao et al. (2021), The Effects of a Self-Similar Avatar Voice in Educational Games.** [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1145/3474665); [author full text](https://web.ics.purdue.edu/~cmousas/papers/jour21-PACMHCI-AvatarVoice.pdf). Read method, Section 5.2/Table 4, and discussion. The 657 analyzed participants in the similar-voice condition completed more puzzles (6.58 vs. 5.47) and played longer (899.75 vs. 743.80 seconds); competence, relatedness, immersion and similarity identification also increased. Direct IMI-subscale differences were not significant. This measures voluntary gameplay and completed puzzles, not faster fixed-task search. The revised introduction states that distinction.
- **Guo et al. (2024), Collaborating with my Doppelgänger.** [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1145/3651288); [author full text](https://web.ics.purdue.edu/~cmousas/papers/jour24-PACMCGIT-SelfSimiarAppearanceVoice.pdf). Read methods/results/discussion in the previously downloaded full text. The 25-participant appearance-by-voice study found higher likability, believability and eeriness for similar voices; appearance and voice interacted for believability. It supplies experience evidence in embodied VR collaboration, not object-search efficiency evidence.
- **Jaggy et al. (2025), AI-determined similarity increases likability and trustworthiness of human voices.** [DOI and full text](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0318890). Read Experiment 5 methods/results. The study relates listener-to-speaker similarity to judgments of recorded other-speaker voices. It does not test synthetic navigation assistants or establish task-performance gains. Added its compact DOI rendering note to the existing entry.
- **Kao et al. (2022), Audio Matters Too: How Audial Avatar Customization Enhances Visual Avatar Customization.** [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1145/3491102.3501848); [author full text](https://people.csail.mit.edu/dkao/pdf/kao2022chi.pdf). Read design and Sections 5.2–5.3. Audial choice improved identification and autonomy conditional on visual choice; no corresponding benefit was found without visual choice. Used to motivate distinguishing choice from resemblance, not to claim that our unembodied voice-selection menu has a demonstrated benefit. Authors, year, proceedings, pages 1–27 and DOI checked against Crossref; metadata snapshot retained here.
